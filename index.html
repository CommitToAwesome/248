Multi-Domain DDIL Decision-Making Trainer

A comprehensive technical specification and functional prototype for training military commanders in Denied, Degraded, Intermittent, and Limited (DDIL) environments.

1. Operational Context & Tactical Requirements

Current military exercises often assume continuous, high-bandwidth connectivity. In a peer-to-peer conflict, Electronic Warfare (EW) and cyber disruption ensure that networks will fail exactly when time-critical decisions are required.

This software trains commanders to lead when digital situational awareness collapses. It does not teach them how to fix the radios; it tests their psychological resilience and adaptability when forced to rely on analog methods, incomplete data, and intuition.

Modern Communications Defense

  • LPI/LPD (Low Probability of Intercept/Detection): Use of mmWave beamforming and signal masking to hide transmissions below the environmental noise floor.
  • Frequency Hopping: Software-Defined Radios (SDR) shifting across hundreds of frequencies per second to avoid narrowband jamming.
  • Self-Healing MANETs: Mobile Ad-Hoc Networks where every soldier and drone acts as a router, ensuring data bypasses severed nodes.
The Core Training Directive: The AI Companion serves as a critical test variable. The simulation will intentionally spoof the AI's data feeds. The trainee is evaluated on their ability to recognize corrupted logic and override the AI, testing for Automation Bias.

2. Simulation Architecture & Chaos Engine

To recreate DDIL conditions, the application cannot use a standard client-server game loop. It utilizes a Degradation Proxy (the Chaos Engine) situated between the Simulation State Server and the Trainee Clients. This proxy actively intercepts, delays, and corrupts packets based on the Instructor's commands and the server's RF physics engine.

3. VoIP Degradation & Digital Signal Processing

Voice comms degradation is handled purely on the client side using the Web Audio API to save server compute. The server sends a lightweight interference value (SINR), and the client manipulates the incoming WebRTC stream through a mathematical DSP node graph.

  • Tactical Handset EQ: A Bandpass filter (300Hz–3kHz) strips high/low frequencies to simulate military radio compression.
  • Cryptographic Desynchronization: An AudioWorklet Bitcrusher artificially reduces bit-depth to simulate failing digital decryption, creating robotic artifacts.
  • Signal Gating: A Low-Frequency Oscillator (LFO) rapidly toggles a Gain node to mimic the harsh stuttering of moving behind terrain.

5. The Instructor Dashboard

The Instructor acts as the adversary. The dashboard consists of three primary zones:

  • The COP (Common Operating Picture): Instructors can toggle between "Ground Truth" (reality) and "Trainee View" (what the trainee actually sees on their jammed screen).
  • Chaos Controls: Drag-and-drop mechanics to apply Area-of-Effect (AoE) broadband jamming over specific grid squares, or targeted cyber injections (e.g., delaying a drone feed by 3 minutes).
  • Comms Matrix: A live node-graph of the squad. Solid green lines represent clear comms, while dashed red lines indicate links severed by EW, allowing the instructor to see isolation in real-time.

6. Automated AAR: The Cognitive DNA Profiler

The After Action Review (AAR) engine logs the "Reality Delta." It maps the trainee's decision-making timeline against the actual ground truth, tracking exact millisecond delays (Decision Latency) and instances of Automation Bias.
